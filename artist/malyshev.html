Hennadiy Malyshev

1922–1999 Landscape and still-life painter

Hennadiy Malyshev (1922–1999) was an Odesa painter and graphic artist who worked mainly in landscape and still life. He was born on 6 March 1922 in the village of Aksai, now in the Volgograd region of Russia, and died in Odesa on 19 April 1999. He fought in the Second World War and received combat awards.

He settled in Odesa in 1946, where he first worked as a club manager. In 1955 he graduated from the Odesa Art School, where he studied under Mykhailo Zhuk, Moisei Mutselmakher, Leonid Muchnyk and Dina Frumina. He exhibited from 1957 and joined the Union of Artists in 1962. From 1965 until his death he worked for the Odesa branch of the Art Fund of Ukraine.

The Encyclopedia of Modern Ukraine places his lyrical landscapes and still lifes in the tradition of the South Russian school of painting. His recurring subjects were the coast at Velykyi Fontan, the countryside around Odesa, the port of Illichivsk, the Dniester and Crimea, and his series "Studies at Velykyi Fontan" spans 1964 to 1998. He also illustrated books, including Russian folk tales (1980). His daughter, Larysa Malysheva, is also an artist.

His works are held by the Odesa and Mykolaiv art museums. A posthumous solo exhibition was held in Odesa in 2000.
